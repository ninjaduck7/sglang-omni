# Qwen3-ASR-1.7B BF16 on a shared desktop RTX 5090 with R570 and CUDA 12.8

## 1. Question and scope

How well does this downstream four-resident service transcribe short English and Mandarin recordings, and what happens to the next client and to GPU resources after an invalid request, disconnect or shutdown? The full-corpus evidence is historical; the fault-injection, duration and lifecycle evidence comes from a separate local experiment. Neither is a CUDA 13 comparison, a long-form or streaming qualification, a TTS/FP8 result, or a commitment by upstream to support this stack.

[Roadmap #1120](https://github.com/sgl-project/sglang-omni/issues/1120) defines support levels, requests reproducible quality and performance evidence, and calls for at least 30 minutes of mixed traffic including failures. [Issue #1212](https://github.com/sgl-project/sglang-omni/issues/1212) is a methodological precedent on a 5090 with R580, CUDA 13, Torch 2.11.0, SGLang 0.5.12.post1 and a 16-resident limit. The subsequent [PR #1213](https://github.com/sgl-project/sglang-omni/pull/1213) retested with Torch 2.13.0 and SGLang 0.5.19. Those are distinct runs, neither on this host.

Single colocated BF16 service, no quantization or CPU offload. FlashInfer decoding, Triton audio attention, CUDA Graph capture batches 1/2/4, torch.compile off, 4 resident requests, 4,096 total KV tokens, static memory fraction 0.65. Eight HTTP clients therefore exercise queueing. Historical base `b83910176`; full-corpus serving SHA `9cfc9e5dbfef072b770f97ae1c7ea37800202698`, published-in-tree report `dcfa287ef`. ## 4. Results by workload and client concurrency

All rows use the four-resident profile. Historical fixed-language rates and P95s are medians of three full-split runs; quality is the range of the three **corpus** rates (sum of edit distances / sum of reference words or normalized Chinese characters, not mean utterance error). The historical auto row is one mixed pass. New rows mix the first 32 rows of each split repeatedly: quality is split-specific across all repetitions, performance is pooled over both languages. The [per-language new CSV](issue-1120/soak-summary.csv) gives the separate counts, P50/P95/P99, edit denominators and CPU. Warmups, control requests and injected faults are excluded from the ordinary request counts. The new cells include time spent serving overlapping probes. HTTP latency includes queuing, upload and response; RTF is mean latency / input duration computed per request; audio s/s is total successful input seconds / wall seconds.
